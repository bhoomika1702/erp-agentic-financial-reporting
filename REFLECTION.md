# One-page reflection — ERP financial reporting take-home

**What I chose and why.** I implemented manual adjustment validation because an invalid journal must be stopped before it contaminates any downstream financial statement. I preferred deterministic `Decimal` arithmetic, approved-COA checks, and explicit `ACCEPTED` / `REVIEW` / `REJECTED` decisions to a convincing but untrustworthy LLM-generated statement. I added a read-only scan of the other company inputs to demonstrate how upstream data quality can block downstream processing.

**What I found in the supplied data.** Of 10 journals, seven passed implemented checks, two were rejected (`JE-002` unbalanced by $3,500; `JE-005` references unknown account `6315`), and one was quarantined for review (`JE-008`, a same-account $320,000 intercompany debit/credit). The current TB includes an unmapped `9999`, repeated `6310`, and multiple currencies; the FX file lacks a GBP period-end rate. The prior TB has legacy account `6905`. These issues are intentionally not silently corrected. Passing journal checks does not establish posting approval or statement correctness.

**With three months.** I would build versioned, entity-aware ledger storage and human approval workflows first; then ERP adapters, a policy-driven FX translation engine, reconciliation/lineage DAG, and a deterministic BS/P&L compiler. Cash flow and SOCIE would follow only after transaction and equity-rollforward metadata were available. An optional retrieval-grounded LLM would rank mapping candidates and draft reviewer explanations; a benchmark would measure accuracy, abstention and reviewer override rates.

**Where it breaks at scale.** The prototype is a single-process CSV/JSON utility, not a transactional service. It does not support thousands of concurrent accounts, entity-scoped mappings, cross-period approval histories, restatements, large IC elimination graphs, streaming ERP deltas, or row-to-cell lineage. Its trace hash detects content changes but is not a signed audit log. Date validation is minimal, and it does not validate the accounting substance or FX calculation behind a balanced journal.

**AI tool use and judgment.** I used ChatGPT to help decompose the requirements, draft the architecture and reflection, propose failure cases, and accelerate Python/test writing. I checked the supplied files and ran the validator rather than trusting generated claims. An important AI failure mode was the temptation to call the same-account intercompany journal a proven circular transaction: without entity/counterparty identifiers, that conclusion is not justified. I therefore classified it as review-required, not automatically invalid. No LLM API is used at runtime.

**What is underestimated.** The hardest problem is the meaning and governance of data, not adding debits and credits. Two equally balanced journals can have radically different accounting validity. COA changes, functional currency, legal-entity ownership, historical rates, period cutoffs and restatement policies all require documented decisions, provenance and accountable human approval.
